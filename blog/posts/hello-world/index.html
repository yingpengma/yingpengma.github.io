This is an example post. It shows how an essay on this blog is set: the date and byline at the top, the title and a one-line summary, and then the body in a single, readable column.

A section#

Each section starts with a heading. Hover over it and a # appears after it, linking straight to the section. Text can carry emphasis, strong emphasis and links.

A quotation is set off with a thin rule on its left.

Lists come in two kinds. A numbered list, for steps or ranked items:

  1. The first item. Items are set like the text around them, with no extra space between them.
  2. The second item, which runs long enough to wrap onto a second line, so you can see how the wrapped line lines up under the first.
  3. The third item.

And a bulleted list, for points in no particular order:

  • A point.
  • Another point, with a link in it.

A figure#

Charts are drawn as SVG in the same ink as the text, on the same paper.

Another section#

When an essay has two or more section headings, a Contents list is built from them. On a wide screen it stays in the left margin and marks the section you are reading; on a narrow one it folds up above the essay.

A subsection#

Subsections are indented under their section in the Contents. A note can be attached to any sentence.1

The essay ends with the seal after its last word.

  1. On a wide screen this note sits in the right margin, beside the sentence it belongs to. On a narrow one it moves here, under a rule at the foot of the essay. ↩︎
